# 0003: Rescue steps

Tech spec for [proposal 0003](https://github.com/itinera-dev/spec/blob/main/proposals/0003-rescue-steps.md), implemented in [#3](https://github.com/itinera-dev/itinera-ts/issues/3). The [tier 1 plan](../tier-1-plan.md) holds what crosses proposals, section 4 the decorators, [0008](0008-steps.md) the steps, [0098](0098-rescue-step-adapters-and-providers.md) the adapters and providers of a rescue step, and [0062](0062-workflow-descriptors.md) the table of requests and the descriptors.

Status: complete, and 3 is listed in `conformance.json`. Stage 4 has added the base class `RescueStep`, `@rescueStep`, `@fromRescuedStep`, and the `rescue` of `@withStep`. Its sixth layer has added the rescue step to the builders, its seventh has added it to the listing and its violations. Stage 5 has added the outcome of a failure. Stage 9 rescues steps in the engine, with test support's rescue step mock, gave the runner its rescue steps and listed the proposal.

## API

Until 0003 is listed, everything here is exported only from `itinera/unstable`. It is defined in `src/step/rescue.ts` and re-exported by `src/step.ts`, which becomes the subpath `itinera/step`.

- **`RescueStep`** is abstract, like `Step`, with its own ECMAScript private brand, `#rescueStep`, so a step is not a rescue step nor a rescue step a step, and `RescueStepClass` is a class extending it. Its `run(...requests)` is abstract and returns a `RescueOutcome` or a promise of one. `RescueOutcome` is a success, made with `this.succeed()`, or a failure, made with `this.fail(reason)`, which takes a reason as a step's does (see [0008](0008-steps.md)), and never a skip, since a rescue step cannot skip, so `this.skip()` does not exist on it and a `Skip` is not a `RescueOutcome`.
- **`@rescueStep()`** takes a class extending `RescueStep` itself and records, in a `WeakMap` keyed by the class, what its constructor and its `run` request, as `@step` does. When the class is defined, it throws if the class extends another rescue step, if it is declared twice, or if a parameter has no request. `requestsOfRescue(class)` reads what it recorded.
- **Its requests.** On the constructor, `@input(key, type?)`, `@fromRescuedStep(key, type?)`, `@failureReason(options?)` and `@error(options?)`; on `run`, `@service(key, serviceType)`, `@contributor()` and `@stepReporter()`. `@fromRescuedStep` reads what the rescued step contributed under its key in the attempt that failed, typed like `@input`, and compiles only on the constructor of a rescue step. The places of the other requests were widened to take a rescue step.
- **`rescue`** in `StepOptions` names a step's rescue step. `@withStep` throws, when the class is defined, for a rescue step not declared with `@rescueStep`, and a class that does not extend `RescueStep` does not compile. `describeStep` keeps the rescue step beside the step's policies, read by `rescueOf(descriptor)` with its name, `<step name>_rescue`, and its requests, which the listing reads. When the workflow is built, a step with both a rescue step and a retry budget greater than 0 is the violation `rescued step retried`, and the rescue step's name counts among the step names for `duplicate step name` (see [0062](0062-workflow-descriptors.md)). In the listing, a step with a rescue step has a rescue section: the rescue step's name, its input adapter, if any, and its services with their providers.
- **The builders.** A step's builder takes its rescue step in `rescue(...)`: a class declared with `@rescueStep`, or a `RescueStepBuilder<C>`, in `src/step/rescue/builder.ts`, which `Refund.builder()`, a static method of `RescueStep`, starts for a class declared without decorators. That builder takes the rescue step's requests by position, with `needs` and `run`, as a step's builder does (see [0062](0062-workflow-descriptors.md)), and each refusal is thrown at once: a class not declared with `@rescueStep`, a class that declares its requests with decorators given them by position, and a parameter with no request.
- **Rescuing a step**, in the private engine. The executor's own rule, `afterFailedAttempt` in `src/engine/decision.ts`, hands the first attempt of a step with a rescue step to it, after a failure or an abnormal termination, whatever the step says about retrying abnormal terminations; the violation `rescued step retried` makes the first attempt the only one before the rescue, so `attempt.isFirst()` is the whole of "not rescued yet". `decided`, in `src/engine/outcome/failed.ts`, emits `step_rescuing` and calls no hook, after `on step abnormal termination` has run for an abnormal termination, whose `FailWorkflow` still gives the step up. The scan, in `src/engine/scan.ts`, then calls `rescued`, in `src/engine/rescue.ts`, which runs the rescue step as its own attempt 1 under `<step name>_rescue`, with the journey's current step set to that name so that every abort while it is built or runs names it, then sets it back to the step before `step_rescued`. A success commits the rescue step's contributions, emits `step_rescued`, and the scan attempts the step again; a failure or an abnormal termination emits `step_given_up` for the rescue step, decided by default, and the journey fails at it; a skip, which only a cast lets through, emits `step_skipped` and `contributions_discarded`, then aborts the journey with `rescue skipped`. Statuses stay private, as for every step: the runner reads them from the events.
- **What the rescue step receives**, in `src/engine/rescue/request.ts`, resolved in the order its constructor declares them, with its inputs: `@fromRescuedStep` reads what the failed attempt contributed, as an input is read, so `optional_input_absent` and the aborts name the rescue step as a step (`requester: { kind: "step" }`), as itinera-rs does; `@failureReason` gives a copy of the reason and `@error` the rescue step's own copy of the error, made as a hook's is, in `src/engine/own-copy.ts`, and a required one that does not exist aborts with `required data missing`, naming the rescue step with `requester: { kind: "rescue step" }`. What a failure request receives is shared with hooks, as `failureAnswered` in `src/engine/request.ts`. An abnormal termination leaves no contributions, as it does for hooks.
- **One shape for a step and a rescue step.** `Buildable`, in `src/engine/buildable.ts`, gives the engine a name, the requests, how to build it and, for a rescue step, what the failed attempt left, so `built`, `servicesFor` and `ran` serve both, and the input adapter and the providers are found under the rescue step's own name (see [0098](0098-rescue-step-adapters-and-providers.md)).
- **Test support.** `rescueStepMock(script)`, in `src/testing/rescue-step-mock.ts`, exported from `itinera/unstable` until 0108 is listed, makes a class of rescue step mocks as `stepMock` does for steps: each plays a `RescueEnding`, in `src/testing/ending.ts`, which is an `Ending` without the skip, so a mock that skips does not compile, may end asynchronously or fail to be built, and keeps what it was built with as `inputs`.

- `tests/unit/engine/outcome/failed.test.ts`: `step_rescuing` with its cause, no retry or failure hook for the rescued attempt, `on step abnormal termination` before it, its `FailWorkflow` giving the step up instead, and the attempt after the rescue given up, calling `on step failure` once.
- `tests/unit/engine/rescue.test.ts`: the rescue step's own attempt 1 and `step_rescued`, its contributions committed before it for the step's next attempt, a rescue step that fails or terminates abnormally given up and failing the journey, its error in the result, no hook of the rescued step called, `rescue skipped` through a cast, and the abort naming the rescue step or the rescued step.
- `tests/unit/engine/rescue/request.test.ts`: the failed attempt's contributions, never committed, and its reason in declared order with the inputs, the error as the rescue step's own copy, an optional contribution absent with `optional_input_absent`, a required reason or error that does not exist, and a contribution of another type.
- `tests/unit/engine/request.test.ts`: what `failureAnswered` gives a request for the failure's reason or the error, and the abort naming its requester. `tests/unit/engine/own-copy.test.ts`: the error's own copy, its class, message, stack, cause and properties kept, a getter copied without being called, and what cannot be copied or is no object given as it is.
- `tests/unit/testing/rescue-step-mock.test.ts` and `.test-d.ts`: the mock's endings, asynchronous run, inputs and build failure, and that it takes no skip.
- `examples/rescue-step.ts` and `examples/rescue-step-mock.ts`.
